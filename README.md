## Overview

This repository contains an anonymized research dataset derived from institutional Moodle learning activities at the **Computer Education Study Program, Faculty of Teacher Training and Education (FKIP), Universitas Mulawarman, Indonesia**.

The dataset was collected from regular teaching and learning activities conducted during the **COVID-19 pandemic and the early transition toward the new-normal learning period**. It therefore represents learner activities within a specific institutional, educational, and temporal context.

The dataset supports the research manuscript:

## Dataset Description

The dataset originates from regular educational activities conducted through the institutional Moodle environment of the **Computer Education Study Program, FKIP Universitas Mulawarman, Indonesia**.

The records cover learning activities during the **COVID-19 pandemic and the early stages of the transition toward new-normal learning**.

During this period, Moodle was used to support regular course activities, including access to learning materials, assignments, quizzes, assessments, and other learning interactions.

The underlying research records include information associated with:

- courses;
- learner accounts;
- Moodle activity logs;
- learning materials;
- assignments;
- quiz attempts;
- assessment-related records;
- learner activity histories;
- Index of Learning Styles (ILS) questionnaire responses; and
- ILS dimensional scores.

The study identified approximately:

- 461 learners with complete ILS questionnaire responses
- multiple courses and academic periods
- learner activities recorded across the COVID-19 and early new-normal learning periods

The dataset made available in this repository represents a research-oriented version of the institutional data prepared for analysis and reproducibility.

## Participant and Record Selection

The questionnaire-linked research cohort was drawn from students of the **Computer Education Study Program, FKIP Universitas Mulawarman** who participated in Moodle-based learning activities during the study period.

For inclusion in the questionnaire-linked dataset, learners were required to:

1. be registered with a student role in the Moodle environment;
2. have recorded learning activity in Moodle;
3. actively participate in Moodle-based course activities;
4. complete the required **44-item Index of Learning Styles (ILS) questionnaire**; and
5. have sufficient relational and temporal information for the analyses described in the associated study.

A total of **461 learners had complete ILS questionnaire responses** and were retained as the questionnaire-linked cohort.

The purpose of this selection was to make it possible to examine Moodle behavioral information together with learning-preference information at the learner level.

## Moodle Learning Activity Data

The Moodle data represent activities generated through regular teaching and learning processes.

Depending on the course and academic period, recorded activities may include interactions with:

- course pages;
- learning resources;
- quizzes;
- assignments;
- assessments;
- learning materials;
- forum or communication activities;
- attendance-related activities; and
- other Moodle components used in the learning process.

The research dataset preserves the relevant relationships among learners, courses, activities, assessments, and questionnaire records required for analysis.

The files released in this repository may contain aggregated, cleaned, or research-oriented representations of the original Moodle records rather than a complete institutional Moodle database export.

---

## Index of Learning Styles (ILS)

The questionnaire component of the dataset uses the **Index of Learning Styles (ILS)** associated with the Felder-Silverman learning-style framework.

The instrument consists of **44 items** representing four learning-preference dimensions:

- **Active – Reflective**
- **Sensing – Intuitive**
- **Visual – Verbal**
- **Sequential – Global**

Each dimension is represented by 11 questionnaire items.

Only learners who completed all required questionnaire responses were included in the questionnaire-linked analytical cohort.

In the associated study, the ILS dimensions are treated as learner preferences rather than fixed indicators of learner ability.

## Data Anonymization and Privacy

The research dataset released through this repository has been **anonymized or pseudonymized** before public dissemination.

Direct personally identifying information is not included in the released analytical dataset.

Information excluded from the research release includes direct identifiers such as:

- student names;
- personal email addresses;
- IP addresses; and
- other direct identifying information.

Anonymous or pseudonymous learner identifiers are used where necessary to preserve analytical relationships among learner activity, courses, assessments, and questionnaire records.

The anonymization process was designed to retain the relational and temporal information required for research while protecting individual learner identities.

Researchers using this dataset must not attempt to identify or re-identify individual learners.

## Research Context

The data were obtained from the **Computer Education Study Program, Faculty of Teacher Training and Education (FKIP), Universitas Mulawarman, Indonesia**.

The observation period includes educational activities conducted during the **COVID-19 pandemic and the early transition toward new-normal learning**.

This period involved substantial use of online and digitally supported learning environments. Moodle was used as part of regular educational activities within the study program.

Consequently, patterns of Moodle activity may reflect the educational circumstances of that period, including:

- online and blended learning practices;
- changes in teaching delivery;
- course-specific learning designs;
- assessment schedules;
- availability of digital learning resources;
- instructor teaching practices;
- learner adaptation to online learning; and
- the transition from pandemic-era arrangements toward early new-normal learning.

Researchers are encouraged to consider this institutional and temporal context when interpreting the dataset or comparing it with datasets collected at other institutions, programs, or academic periods.

## Important Research Considerations

This dataset originates from a single academic program and a particular historical learning period.

Researchers should therefore avoid assuming that patterns observed in this dataset automatically represent:

- all students at Universitas Mulawarman;
- students from other study programs;
- students from other institutions;
- all Moodle environments;
- all online-learning periods; or
- present-day learning behavior.

The dataset is best understood as a record of Moodle-supported educational activity within the **Computer Education Study Program at FKIP Universitas Mulawarman during the COVID-19 period and the early transition toward new-normal learning**.

Independent validation using other cohorts, institutions, and academic periods is encouraged.
